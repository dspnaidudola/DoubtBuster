Unit 2: Linear Algebra & Matrix Eigenvalues
Course: Engineering Mathematics (GMRIT Undergraduate Curriculum)

Section 1: Characteristic Equation
For any square matrix A of size n x n, the scalar lambda is an eigenvalue of A if there exists a non-zero vector v such that:
A * v = lambda * v

This can be rearranged into the homogeneous linear system:
(A - lambda * I) * v = 0

For non-trivial solutions (v != 0) to exist, the determinant of the coefficient matrix must be zero:
det(A - lambda * I) = 0

This determinant equation is called the Characteristic Equation of matrix A.
The roots of this polynomial equation are the eigenvalues of A.

Section 2: Finding Eigenvectors
To find the eigenvector corresponding to each eigenvalue lambda:
1. Substitute lambda into (A - lambda * I) * v = 0.
2. Solve the resulting system of linear equations using row reduction (Gaussian elimination).
3. Express the solution vector in terms of free parameters.
